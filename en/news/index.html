Sunday, November 22, 2026 · 5 a.m. Japan time edition

Middle East key stories, from regional media and YouTube reports

  1. 1
    Strait of Hormuz: Iran says it shot down two US drones; two tankers also hit
    • Iran's Revolutionary Guard said on the 21st that it shot down a US MQ-9 drone over the Strait of Hormuz and an MQ-1 over Qeshm Island. Iran's army also claims it hit a US F-16 fighter jet, but US Central Command denies this.
    • The UK maritime agency UKMTO says two tankers leaving the strait were hit by unidentified projectiles off Oman on the 21st. Separately, Iran's navy said a tanker east of the strait was on fire after hitting a sea mine.
    • The US and Iran signed a memorandum in June, but talks are stuck over whether ships can pass freely through the strait. On the 20th, President Trump warned Iran, hinting at military action, over a reported "dirty bomb" plot (Iran calls the report a fabrication). On the 21st, President Trump said he wants a "permanent" deal to stop Iran getting nuclear weapons.
    Why it matters: the Strait of Hormuz is a main route for the world's oil. The longer the fighting lasts, the more it affects oil prices and shipping.
    Sources: Al Jazeera (Middle East) / Yahoo News (Al Jazeera) / Investing.com / AOL (AP) / Newscord (9 outlets compared) / Middle East Monitor / Il Sole 24 Ore / YouTube (Al Jazeera English) / YouTube (TRT World) / YouTube (Middle East Eye)
  2. 2
    Saudi-led coalition strikes Yemen's capital Sanaa and other areas; Houthis fire missiles at Riyadh
    • After Hodeidah port, the Saudi-led coalition also struck the capital Sanaa, Saada and Taiz. It says it hit "military targets" such as missile and drone facilities and a supply depot.
    • The Houthis (an Iran-aligned armed group in Yemen) said 41 people were killed in the strikes over the past 24 hours. This is the Houthis' own figure and has not been confirmed by others.
    • The Houthis said they struck targets across Saudi Arabia, including Riyadh. The coalition said it shot down three ballistic missiles heading for Riyadh. In Taiz province, ground fighting is also reported to be getting worse while aid funding falls.
    Why it matters: fighting between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis had been quiet for months and has suddenly become intense. Worry is spreading to Red Sea ports and air travel.
    Sources: The New Arab / Al Jazeera / Gulf News / Business Standard / Yahoo News / Courthouse News / YouTube (Al Jazeera English) / YouTube (TRT World)
  3. 3
    Gaza: Israeli strike hits a vehicle carrying food, killing threeLittle covered in Japan
    • Gaza's health ministry and witnesses say an Israeli strike hit a vehicle loaded with food in the Tel al-Hawa area of Gaza City on the 21st, killing three people.
    • The Israeli military says the vehicle was being used by members of the armed group Islamic Jihad to move food and supplies to other fighters. Papers with the logo of the aid group World Central Kitchen were seen at the scene, but the group says the vehicle was not theirs.
    • Gaza's Civil Defence says one more person was killed by gunfire in Jabalia in the north. More than 85% of the Civil Defence's vehicles are reported to be out of service. The head of a Palestinian NGO network says more than 150,000 tents are being held back by Israel, and tents in displacement camps are flooding in the winter rain.
    Why it matters: in a Gaza that is supposed to be under a ceasefire, delivering food and rescuing people both remain difficult.
    Sources: Al Jazeera / Arab News / TRT World / NBC News (Reuters) / New Straits Times / YouTube (Al Jazeera English) / YouTube (Middle East Eye) / YouTube (Middle East Eye, tents)
  4. 4
    West Bank: settler attacks surge; a Palestinian man is killedLittle covered in Japan
    • Al Jazeera reports a wave of attacks by Israeli settlers in the occupied West Bank, in which at least one Palestinian man was killed.
    • The Israeli military has also launched new raids across the West Bank.
    • Middle East Eye shows video of armed settlers surrounding a Palestinian home, and of settlers taking boxes of fruit and vegetables from a vendor.
    Why it matters: while attention is on Gaza, people's lives and safety are also under threat in the West Bank.
    Sources: YouTube (Al Jazeera English) / YouTube (Middle East Eye) / YouTube (Middle East Eye, vendor)
  5. 5
    Iraq: government forces enter Jurf al-Sakhar, stronghold of an Iran-backed armed groupLittle covered in Japan
    • Iraqi security forces entered Jurf al-Sakhar, south of Baghdad, on the 20th. It is a stronghold of the Iran-backed group Kataib Hezbollah. A deal between the government and the group avoided a clash.
    • The move followed a government investigation that confirmed oil smuggling in the area. It is reported to be still unclear whether the group will really give up control.
    • Separately, a large fire broke out on the 21st at the Hartha power station north of Basra in the south. Security officials say at least four people were killed and 30 injured.
    Why it matters: this is a test of whether Iraq's government can rein in Iran-backed armed groups.
    Sources: Al Jazeera / YouTube (Middle East Eye) / Rudaw / Kurdistan24 / YouTube (Middle East Eye, fire) / YouTube (TRT World, fire)
  6. 6
    Israel: opposition leader Eisenkot halfway through his time to form a new governmentLittle covered in Japan
    • Middle East Eye reports that opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot is about halfway through the period he has to form a new government, and coalition talks continue.
    • Mr Netanyahu is reported to be attacking the country's Arab parties and arguing for another round of war with Iran.
    • Inside Israel, ultra-Orthodox protesters clashed with police after a religious student who had not enlisted in the army was arrested.
    Sources: YouTube (Middle East Eye) / YouTube (Middle East Eye, protest)
  7. 7
    Bahrain: an Iranian attack sets an industrial site on fire; Iran says it targeted US bases
    • Bahrain's Interior Ministry said late on the 21st that "a civilian industrial facility was targeted" in an Iranian attack, causing a fire. No injuries were reported, only material damage, and civil defence crews worked to put out the blaze. The ministry did not name the site (some reports said smoke was seen over Sitra Island, home to a refinery).
    • Bahrain's military said it intercepted a number of Iranian aerial attacks. Authorities issued another "potential threat" alert and urged calm.
    • Iran's Revolutionary Guard said it struck US military positions in Bahrain, and on the 22nd claimed it hit two US Patriot radars. Bahrain rejected this and asked the UN Security Council to act. Jordan and the GCC condemned the attack.
    • The same day, Guard commander Ahmad Vahidi said in a video that the Gulf usually had 70 to 80, at times up to 120, Western naval vessels, but none had been seen for months, and that Iran's enemies had "no choice but to admit defeat" and leave the region.
    Why it matters: the Hormuz conflict has now reached factories on the other side of the Gulf, affecting life there and the flow of oil.
    Sources: YouTube (Al Jazeera English) / Saudi Gazette / Arab Times / Xinhua / Iran International / YouTube (Middle East Eye, commander)
  8. 8
    Iran's president: "a silent economic war, harder than a war of missiles"Little covered in Japan
    • Speaking to officials working on the economy, President Masoud Pezeshkian said: "We are engaged in a silent war that is far more difficult than a war in which missiles are launched and destruction is inflicted."
    • "It is an economic war," he added, "and it is by no means an easy one."
    Why it matters: alongside the fighting, ordinary Iranians are being squeezed economically.
    Sources: YouTube (Al Jazeera English)
  9. 9
    Gaza: the "Yellow Line" keeps moving and families lose their homes; pallets become classroomsLittle covered in Japan
    • The mayor of Al-Maghazi says more than 500 homes in eastern areas were destroyed in under a month. Al Jazeera's reporter says the "Yellow Line" marking Israeli-controlled territory keeps shifting west, and even without ground incursions there is daily shelling, demolition and drone attacks. A house destroyed two days earlier had sheltered five families.
    • With building materials blocked, a carpenter takes apart discarded wooden pallets to make furniture: "this wood has become our closest ally," he says. A school has used pallets to build classrooms so lessons can go on.
    • In the ruins of Gaza City's Al-Nasr Cinema, the Gaza Children's Film Festival was held under the banner "Tomorrow We Love Life", with children sitting on sand atop the rubble. Nearly all of Gaza's roughly 2 million people remain displaced.
    Why it matters: even after the ceasefire, more people are losing their homes, while others fight to keep working and learning.
    Sources: YouTube (Al Jazeera English) / YouTube (TRT World, pallets) / YouTube (TRT World, film festival)
  10. 10
    Southern Lebanon: beekeepers watch the sky for drones while saving their beesLittle covered in Japan
    • Beekeepers in southern Lebanon must now watch the skies as well as their hives. Dozens have been killed in Israeli strikes since the latest war began, not on a battlefield but on their way to their hives.
    • Filmmaker and beekeeper Hassan Haidar stayed in his village when others fled, filming his effort to save his hives as fellow beekeepers were killed, as a tribute to them.
    • Zainab, who lost her husband, now supports her family and keeps his hives: "I had to be strong for the children and for the bees, because he loved them both." Drones can hover for an hour before striking: "It is frightening, but the buzzing of the bees is louder."
    Why it matters: war keeps taking people's work and income long after the fighting peaks.
    Sources: YouTube (Al Jazeera English)
  11. 11
    Syria: Idlib has lost over 1.5 million trees; temperatures up nearly 2°C in a decade, and floods kill eightLittle covered in Japan
    • More than 1.5 million trees in Idlib were cut down or burned under the Assad government. Returning home, Abu Mahmood found all his trees gone: "We tried to restore them, but the area had gone through a severe drought."
    • Wildfires burned about 100 sq km in coastal Syria, destroying hundreds of thousands of trees. Temperatures have risen nearly 2°C over the past decade, and water is scarcer.
    • This year also brought heavy rain and floods that killed eight people, including five children, and forced hundreds of families to evacuate. Rebuilding now has to plan drainage for changing rainfall.
    Why it matters: post-war rebuilding risks falling behind a fast-changing climate.
    Sources: YouTube (TRT World)

World top 5 stories most widely covered

  1. 1
    US stocks plunge: Nasdaq down 5.28% in a day; S&P 500 and Dow now more than 10% below their highs
    • In New York on the 20th, the Nasdaq fell 5.28%, the S&P 500 fell 3.70% and the Dow fell 949 points (1.93%). It was the S&P 500's seventh fall in a row.
    • AI and chip companies were sold, and Nvidia fell about 14%. The S&P 500 lost 8.6% over the week, its worst week since April 2025.
    • The Nasdaq has now lost all of its gains for this year. Gold was sold too, falling to nearly $3,500 per troy ounce.
    Why it matters: shares that rose on hopes for AI are falling at the same time around the world. This will affect markets in Japan and Asia after the weekend, as well as pensions and investment funds.
    Sources: CNBC / UPI / FINCHANNEL (AP) / 24/7 Wall St. / StockTitan / Kitco
  2. 2
    Russia and Europe: Denmark expels Russia's defence attaché; objects also enter Polish airspace
    • Denmark's intelligence service PET said on the 20th that a plot in September to kill a rapper who holds Ukrainian and US citizenship was planned by Russian intelligence. Denmark has decided to expel Russia's defence attaché. Russia denies it, calling the accusation unsubstantiated.
    • Ukraine's Prime Minister Koretskyi says eight people were killed and 28 wounded in Russia's massive attack on the 19th–20th. Bridges in Kyiv and power facilities were damaged. TRT World reports that a Russian strike in Kryvyi Rih also killed a seven-year-old boy and a paramedic and injured at least 47 people.
    • Poland said three objects (thought to be two missiles and a drone) entered its airspace during the attack.
    Why it matters: right after the US–China–Russia summit, the war is reaching European countries beyond Ukraine.
    Sources: Al Jazeera (Denmark) / CNN / Yahoo News / RTÉ / Al Jazeera (Ukraine) / UPI / US News (Reuters) / YouTube (TRT World) / YouTube (Al Jazeera English)
  3. 3
    COP31 climate talks in Antalya, Türkiye, run into overtime; the words "fossil fuels" vanish from the draft deal
    • The talks passed their final day on the 20th without agreement. The new draft does not mention fossil fuels.
    • France, Panama and others want the words put back, while oil producers such as Saudi Arabia and Russia are against it. African countries are asking for grants rather than loans.
    • Ethiopia was confirmed as host of next year's COP32.
    Why it matters: this will show whether the promise to move away from oil and coal is slipping backwards.
    Sources: UN News / Yahoo News / Forbes / The Climate Watch / YouTube (TRT World) / YouTube (TRT World, Africa)
  4. 4
    Mexico: cartel boss "El Chapo Isidro" arrested; gunfire and burning trucks in Sinaloa
    • Mexican special forces arrested Fausto Isidro Meza-Flores, who was on the FBI's Ten Most Wanted list.
    • Gun battles and burning trucks followed in the state of Sinaloa. Spectators at a nearby baseball stadium fled in panic.
    • Mexico's government says troops will stay in Sinaloa to protect civilians.
    Sources: Al Jazeera / NPR / CBS News / NBC News / YouTube (TRT World)
  5. 5
    World Court rules that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya
    • The UN's top court, the International Court of Justice, ruled on the 19th that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya minority. The judges voted nine to six.
    • It is the first time the court has held a state responsible for genocide. The Gambia brought the case seven years ago.
    • The ruling is final and cannot be appealed. The amount of compensation will be decided later.
    Why it matters: it is expected to affect other cases, including South Africa's case against Israel.
    Sources: Human Rights Watch / OPB (NPR) / CBS News / Anadolu / YouTube (Middle East Eye)
