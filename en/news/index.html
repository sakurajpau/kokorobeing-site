Spain: a tanker carrying diesel turns over near the island of Ibiza; one crew member dies
On the 26th, about 25 nautical miles (about 46 km) northeast of the island of Ibiza in the Mediterranean Sea, the Malta-flagged tanker “Med Emre” turned over after reporting that water had come in during a storm. Sources: Yahoo News / Yahoo News (worries about sea life) / Splash247 / YouTube (Al Jazeera English)
UK: the number of people moving in is the lowest in about 30 years, but two in three people think it is “going up”
On the 26th, the UK's statistics office said that in the year to June this year, the number of people who came in minus the number who left (net migration) was 77,000. Leaving out the Covid period, this is the lowest number since 1997. Three years ago it was nearly 1 million. The reason is that visa rules have become stricter. However, in a survey by King's College London, only 4% of people correctly answered that it is “going down,” and 63% thought it is “going up.” Economists say that if the number of people moving in keeps falling, there may not be enough workers.
Sources: Express Tribune (Reuters) / YouTube (Al Jazeera English)
Brazil, China, Singapore and others push back against the new US tariff plan
On the 25th, the Trump administration set out a plan to put new tariffs of up to 20% on goods from 14 countries and regions, including China and the EU. They replace the earlier tariffs, which the Supreme Court ruled in February the president “does not have the power” to impose, and they are expected to start early next year. Brazil says the plan is “unacceptable,” China says the US is “using it as a political tool,” and Singapore says it is “very disappointing.” Five countries and regions in Asia face tariffs of up to 18%. Experts think these tariffs will also be fought over in court.
Sources: Al Jazeera / The Globe and Mail / Al Jazeera (Inside Story)

Japan top 5 little covered by the big networks, but worth knowing

  1. 1
    Long-term care insurance: no decision on widening the group who pay “20%,” and talks will continue; the review of premiums for people aged 65 and over is broadly accepted
    On the 26th, a Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare council (the Long-Term Care Insurance Subcommittee) discussed how to share the cost of care. According to care industry news, the review of the premium levels paid by people aged 65 and over was broadly accepted.     Sources: Japan Day Care Research Association / Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (Long-Term Care Insurance Subcommittee) / Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (papers) / GemMed
  2. 2
    Next spring's unified local elections: six prefectures move toward ordinances for “electronic voting” on tablets and similar devices; 36 prefectures are “not considering it”
    According to a Kyodo News survey (November 23), six prefectures have made, or are trying to make, an ordinance with the aim of “electronic voting” in the unified local elections next April. This means voting on a machine instead of writing on paper. Kagawa and Gifu prefectures have already made one. Chiba Prefecture put a plan to its assembly on November 20, Kanagawa and Toyama prefectures plan to do so within this month, and Fukuoka Prefecture is also considering it. Four other prefectures say it is difficult this time but they want to bring it in in the future. 36 prefectures answered that they are “not considering it now.” To use it in elections for governor or the prefectural assembly, both the prefecture and the cities, towns and villages need an ordinance. Why it is worth knowing: fewer votes will be thrown out because of writing mistakes, and counting will be faster. On the other hand, there also needs to be a plan for machine trouble. It is worth knowing what your own prefecture will do.
    Sources: Tokyo Shimbun (Kyodo) / Shikoku Shimbun (Kyodo) / Toyama Shimbun

    On the 26th, Aichi Prefecture said the culling of about 82,000 egg-laying chickens was finished at a chicken farm in Toyohashi where bird flu had been confirmed. Through the winter, the time for taking care continues in many places.

    Trouble over funeral costs is rising; the National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan says “always get a written estimate, and do not decide alone”
    On November 19, in a notice for people who watch over older people, the National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan took up trouble over funeral costs. There are more and more complaints like “I was asked for a higher price than I expected, and I cannot accept it.” Even with names like “family funeral” or “one-day funeral,” what is included differs from one funeral company to another. Food and health from Dr Toshiaki Yoshino's videos

Dr Yoshino: “Even if it is organic, that does not make it fine to eat too much”
In his video of November 25, Dr Toshiaki Yoshino mentions reports that the singer Mayo Shono has had malignant lymphoma (a kind of blood cancer), and says the following. According to Dr Yoshino, after it was found in 2021, Ms. Shono had anti-cancer drug treatment, it was reported in 2022 that the tumor had gone, and this October she held a concert for the 50th year since her debut. Dr Yoshino says clearly that he “will not say” why Ms. Shono became ill. He then said, as something he feels from seeing his own patients, that among the people who come to him with malignant lymphoma, many eat a lot of sweets and fried food, and many take a lot of olive oil, dairy products, fruit juice and the like because they think these are good for the body. Dr Yoshino's explanation is that the oil we eat enters the body through the lymph vessels. Dr Yoshino's view is: “Organic food (food grown without farm chemicals and the like) is not bad in itself. But being organic does not make it fine to take too much oil, sweet things, wheat or dairy products.” Dr Yoshino also says the number of people who get malignant lymphoma has grown to a little over three times what it was 30 years ago.
These are Dr Yoshino's own views (we have not checked the numbers or the explanations of how the body works; this summary is based on the video's automatic captions). Please talk to your own doctor before deciding anything about your body.
Sources: YouTube (Dr Toshiaki Yoshino's channel)

A heartwarming story one gentle story to end with

UK: a cat with a pipe stuck around its neck is rescued and returns to its owner after eight months
In Barking, east of London, a young cat was found with a pipe stuck over its head and around its neck. It ran away whenever people came near, so rescuing it was hard. Today's Mindfulness Let's Practice Mindfulness

Three minutes of sending kind words, for when you feel "there is nothing I can do"

This morning brought stories of cancer patients in Gaza who still cannot get treatment, of people fighting Ebola, and of people who lost their lives while working in a country far from home. Reading them, you may have felt your strength drain away, thinking, "There is nothing I can do." We feel this way because we have a kind heart that cannot look away from someone else's pain.
For just three minutes, let's send kind words in our minds.

  1. Sit in a comfortable position. Close your eyes, or let them rest softly on a spot below you. Take three slow breaths.
  2. First, send words to yourself. Say them slowly in your mind: "May I be at peace."
  3. Next, picture one person who is dear to you. "May you be at peace."
  4. Then, picture the people in this morning's news. It is fine if you cannot see their faces. "May you all be at peace. May your suffering become even a little lighter."
  5. Finally, take one more slow breath and notice how your body feels now. Gently open your eyes, and finish.

Even when we cannot change something, we can always wish for someone's happiness. That wish carries on, as kindness toward the people we meet today.

Why?
When we feel there is nothing we can do, the heart tends to close and grow tired. Saying kind words gently turns the heart from "what I cannot do" toward "what I can wish for." Being kind to yourself first also makes it easier to keep caring about others for a long time.
