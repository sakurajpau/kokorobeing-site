  1. 1
    In the mountains of Gifu Prefecture, a power cut caused by snow enters its sixth day; more than 2,600 homes in Gujo and Takayama
    Heavy, wet snow from around November 20 brought down many trees and cut power lines, and the power cut in the mountains of Gifu Prefecture is continuing.   2. 2
    Aomori Prefecture: bear reports are at 40% of last year's level; the beech nut crop and last year's culling may be the reasons
    According to the To-o Nippo newspaper, there were 1,283 reports of Asian black bears in Aomori Prefecture this year up to November 24 (sightings, attacks on people, and traces found). That is only about 40% of the number at the same time last year. There were 770 reports in October last year, but 46 in October this year. People involved think the reasons are that beech nuts, which bears eat, were an “average crop” this year, and that many bears were culled last year. According to early figures from the Ministry of the Environment, 238 people across Japan were attacked by bears in fiscal 2025 and 13 people died, both the highest numbers ever. Why it is worth knowing: the number of bears coming into places where people live changes a lot from year to year, depending on how much food there is in the mountains. Even in a year when the number is down, we cannot let our guard down.
    Sources: To-o Nippo / nippon.com (Ministry of the Environment summary)

Food and health from Dr Toshiaki Yoshino's videos

Dr Yoshino: why were school lunches bread and milk? His suggestion is “Japanese food”
In a talk with Kazuyasu Ishida (November 25), Dr Toshiaki Yoshino says the following. According to Dr Yoshino, school lunches in Japan after the war came to be built around bread and milk because of lobbying by the US wheat and milk industries and the policy of GHQ, which was occupying Japan. Dr Yoshino also says that much of the bread sold in Japan contains sugar, vegetable oil, skim milk powder and margarine, and is “close to a sweet.” Dr Yoshino recommends cutting back on sugar, wheat, vegetable oil and dairy products, and his view is that these changes in how people eat are linked to the rise in illness. Asked what people should eat, then, he answered, “Japanese food is fine,” and gave sashimi, grilled fish, and hot pot with seasonal foods as examples. Dr Yoshino explains that people want fried food because the body is looking for a “browned smell,” and that grilled fish and similar foods can also satisfy this. On alcohol, Dr Yoshino says people whose bodies cannot take it must not drink, and people who can drink should do so in moderation. Dr Yoshino also says he tells people with cancer to get standard treatment without waiting.
These are Dr Yoshino's own views (we have not checked them). Please talk to your own doctor before deciding anything about your body.
Sources: YouTube (越境3.0チャンネル, talk with Dr Toshiaki Yoshino)

Brazil: a 74-year-old man missing for 11 days in the Amazon forest is found safe
In the state of Amapá in northern Brazil, Janarino Ferreira dos Santos, a 74-year-old man with Alzheimer's disease, went missing in the Amazon forest. Today's Mindfulness Let's Practice Mindfulness

Three minutes with a hand on your chest, after news that makes your heart ache

This morning brought the story of a family of four who were killed at home, and of women whose lives are taken by family members or partners. Reading them, you may have felt a tightness in your chest. We feel sad because we have a heart that cares about others.
For just three minutes, let's gently place a hand on our own chest.

  1. Sit in a comfortable position. Softly place one hand, or both hands, on the middle of your chest.
  2. Feel the warmth of your hand, and the weight of your hand. Notice your chest rising and falling with your breath.
  3. Breathe in slowly, and breathe out slowly. Do this five times. Let the out-breath be a little longer.
  4. In your mind, speak to yourself. "This is sad." "It's all right to feel this." It is fine if the words do not come out well.
  5. Finally, for the length of one breath, wish that the people in the news may have even a little peace. Gently lower your hand, and finish.

You do not need to make the sadness go away. While your hand is resting there, you are right beside yourself.

When
You have read painful news and your chest feels tight with sadness
Try this
Placing a hand on your chest (soothing touch)
Why?
When we are sad, the body tends to become stiff and the breath tends to become shallow. The warmth of your hand tells your body, "It's all right to be here," just like when someone touches you gently. This practice is not for getting rid of sadness. It is for being kind to the you who is feeling sad.
