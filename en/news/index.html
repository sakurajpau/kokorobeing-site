Middle East key stories, from regional media and YouTube reports

  1. 1
    Strait of Hormuz: tanker attacks continue as mediator Qatar visits Tehran
    • The UK maritime agency UKMTO said a tanker was hit in the stern by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on the 21st. Iran's navy said a different ship caught fire after striking a mine.
    • Talks between the United States and Iran remain stalled. A delegation from mediator Qatar visited Tehran. Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said reopening the strait would be "not difficult" if the US accepted Iran's conditions, and that talks about the strait are pointless until Iran's demands are met. The conditions include ending the US blockade of Iranian ports and lifting oil sanctions.
    • President Trump says he wants a "permanent" deal that keeps Iran from getting a nuclear weapon. On the 18th he said there were signs Iran was looking at chemical or biological weapons, but gave no evidence. Iran denies it. Bahrain renewed its alert against Iranian attacks.
    Why it matters: the Strait of Hormuz is a main route for the world's oil. While talks are stuck, oil prices and shipping stay under pressure.
    Sources: Al Jazeera / Al Jazeera (analysis) / France 24 / Business Recorder (AFP) / Times of Israel / Gulf News / Anadolu / Türkiye Today / CBS News
  2. 2
    Yemen: Houthis claim strike on Saudi refinery and close in on the southern city of Aden
    • The Houthis (a Yemeni armed group close to Iran) claimed on the 22nd a "direct hit" on the Rabigh refinery on Saudi Arabia's Red Sea coast. Saudi authorities have not commented and no damage has been confirmed.
    • The Saudi-led coalition said it shot down a ballistic missile heading for western Saudi Arabia and a drone aimed at Riyadh. The Houthis reported an attack on the capital Sanaa.
    • Al Jazeera reports that the Houthis are said to have advanced inland to about 80 km from Aden. Aden is the base of the internationally recognised government and home to more than one million people. Government forces have pulled back from positions near the Bab al-Mandeb Strait to defend the city.
    • On the Japanese YouTube channel Ekkyo 3.0, commentator Kazuyasu Ishida explained that Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces pulled back from the Bab el-Mandeb Strait and the Houthis took control of it again. A Houthi official said on November 16 that the group had "taken control of the strait, so our goal is achieved, and we have no intention of advancing on Aden", but Ishida believes their actual moves say the opposite. He also said a Houthi attack damaged the headquarters of Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund (PIF) in Riyadh, and that this matters a lot for Japan, which buys oil from Saudi Arabia.
    Why it matters: about 12% of the world's sea trade passes through the Bab al-Mandeb Strait. After Hormuz, shipping here is at risk too.
    Sources: Al Jazeera / Middle East Eye / US News (Reuters) / Yahoo News / Manila Times (AFP) / Al Jazeera (Aden) / The New Arab / YouTube (Al Jazeera English)／YouTube (Ekkyo 3.0 Channel)
  3. 3
    West Bank: settlers attack villages overnight, setting cars on fire one after anotherLittle covered in Japan
    • From the night of the 21st into the 22nd, Israeli settlers attacked the villages of Deir as-Sudan, Deir Abu Mash'al and Kafr Malik near Ramallah. Middle East Eye says 12 vehicles in all were burned and racist graffiti was left on the walls of homes. Some reports count four villages, including one near Hebron in the south.
    • TRT World reports that in Kafr Malik about 12 people came at around 2 a.m. and set fire to cars parked outside homes. More than 80 residents, including children, were left terrified. "We have been attacked before and we no longer feel safe," one resident said. Residents say 15 settler outposts surround the village.
    • Israeli opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot strongly condemned the arson as "Jewish terrorism". Israeli authorities are reported to have opened an investigation.
    Why it matters: settler attacks in the West Bank now happen almost daily, and people can no longer sleep in peace.
    Sources: Middle East Eye / Yahoo News (AFP) / UPI / The Media Line / Times of Israel / YouTube (TRT World) / YouTube (Middle East Eye) / YouTube (Middle East Eye, residents)
  4. 4
    Gaza: displaced people protest as air strikes continueLittle covered in Japan
    • Al Jazeera reports that in Deir el-Balah in central Gaza, displaced people sheltering near the "Yellow Line" (the edge of the area held by the Israeli military) protested against the growing number of air strikes.
    • The head of Gaza's civil defence says 233 people are still missing under the rubble. On the 21st at least four people were killed, three of them in a strike on a vehicle carrying food (according to Gaza's health ministry and others).
    Why it matters: a ceasefire is supposed to be in place, yet displaced people in Gaza have nowhere they can feel safe.
    Sources: Al Jazeera / Al Jazeera (Nov 21) / Al Jazeera (video)
  5. 5
    Lebanon: on the 83rd Independence Day, the president says Israel must withdrawLittle covered in Japan
    • Lebanon marked 83 years of independence from France on the 22nd. In a speech the day before, President Joseph Aoun demanded that Israel withdraw from "every inch" of Lebanese territory. He also criticised interference by elements within Iran, and said, "The time for weapons outside the authority of the state is over."
    • The UN peacekeeping force UNIFIL has begun handing some of its positions in the south to the Lebanese army. UNIFIL's mission is due to end in December. Israeli strikes hit Nabatieh and other parts of the south again on the 21st.
    • TRT World reports that more than 5% of the country is still occupied by Israel. Some people in the south ask, "Is there really independence?" and are staying away from the celebrations.
    Why it matters: people still cannot return to the south after the fighting, and the unity of the country is being tested.
    Sources: The New Arab / Al Jazeera / LBCI / Arab News Japan / Profile News (UNIFIL) / YouTube (TRT World)
  6. 6
    Iraq widens its crackdown on oil smuggling; about 60,000 barrels a day reportedly smuggledLittle covered in Japan
    • Al Jazeera reports that Iraq's Federal Integrity Commission documented more than 200 illicit oil operations across the country between 2024 and 2026. Local reports say about 60,000 barrels are smuggled every day, costing the country nearly $2 billion a year.
    • The government has launched a crackdown, firing several officials accused of involvement and referring others to the courts. People welcome the move but say they still need to see concrete results.
    • Oil brings in more than 90% of Iraq's budget. Exports have fallen sharply because of the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, and the government recently devalued its currency.
    Why it matters: in a country that depends on oil, smuggling and the closed strait are hurting people's lives twice over.
    Sources: YouTube (Al Jazeera English) / Al Jazeera (background)
  7. 7
    Syria plans to close its displacement camps by next September; families rebuild damaged homes on their ownLittle covered in Japan
    • Al Jazeera reports that the Syrian government plans to have no displacement camps by September next year. In Atma in the north, once the country's largest camp, shelters are being taken down block by block and schools have closed.
    • The government is asking aid agencies to stop providing services in the camps, and promises that people who leave voluntarily will be first in line for rebuilding. But there is no national reconstruction plan, and most people are rebuilding on their own. "The residents of the camps have been forgotten," said one person who remains.
    • The World Bank estimates that rebuilding Syria will cost $216 billion.
    Why it matters: the war is over, but the money and help needed to repair people's homes are not there.
    Sources: YouTube (Al Jazeera English)

    UK foreign secretary: "increasing evidence of war crimes" in Gaza; genocide question is for the International Court of JusticeLittle covered in Japan
    • Middle East Eye reports that UK Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said, "I am deeply worried about Israel's activities in Gaza," adding that there is increasing evidence of war crimes. Asked whether it amounts to genocide, he said that is a matter the International Court of Justice is looking at.
    • He called the court's ruling on the 19th that Myanmar committed genocide "a very, very important ruling" and said the UK had expelled Myanmar's military attaché. He said he would announce further measures this week.
    Why it matters: the Myanmar ruling is starting to shape what governments say about Gaza.
    Sources: YouTube (Middle East Eye)
  9. 9
    Israel: Eisenkot, not Netanyahu, asked to form a government; reports say the US and Israel are considering new strikes on IranLittle covered in Japan
    • According to commentator Kazuyasu Ishida on the YouTube channel Ekkyo 3.0, in Israel's general election Prime Minister Netanyahu's camp won 53 seats in total, former army chief Gadi Eisenkot's camp won 48, and Arab parties won 19. The Knesset has 120 seats, so 61 are needed to form a government.
    • He reports that the task of forming the new government was given not to Netanyahu but to Eisenkot, who leads the new party Yashar (21 seats). Netanyahu's camp is still negotiating with other parties.
    • Ishida also pointed to a CBS News report that the US and Israel are considering new strikes on Iran. President Trump has indicated he will think about any new attack after the new year, and Ishida says there is a gap between him and Israel, which wants to move quickly.
    Why it matters: Ishida sees Netanyahu's possible loss of power and the talk of new strikes on Iran as connected. If the war widens again, it will affect daily life in Japan through oil prices.
    Sources: YouTube (Ekkyo 3.0 Channel)

World top 5 stories most widely covered

  1. 1
    COP31 climate summit ends without a deal after a split over the words "fossil fuels"
    • COP31, held in Antalya, Türkiye, ended on the 22nd without an agreed final text. COP31 President Murat Kurum acknowledged at the closing session that the decisions had not been adopted.
    • The final draft did not mention coal, oil or gas. The EU, the UK and island nations such as Tuvalu refused to accept it. Oil-producing countries such as Saudi Arabia and Russia had opposed any wording on fossil fuels.
    • Al Jazeera reports this is the first UN climate summit to end without a deal since 2009, and the longest ever. UN Secretary-General António Guterres said he was "deeply disappointed".
    Why it matters: the world's promise to move away from oil and coal can no longer move forward.
    Sources: CNN / NPR / Al Jazeera / Phys.org (AFP) / Yahoo News / YouTube (Al Jazeera English) / YouTube (TRT World)
  2. 2
    Ukraine and Russia: drone hits apartment block near Kyiv; Russian ships attacked in the Baltic and in Libya
    • On the morning of the 22nd a Russian drone hit a residential building in Dymer, in the Kyiv region. President Zelenskiy said at least 14 people may be trapped under the rubble. Nine people were evacuated. TRT World reports that rescuers recovered two bodies.
    • On the 21st the Russian cargo ship Sparta II caught fire in the Gulf of Finland after a drone attack, and all 16 crew were moved to another ship. The Finnish and Estonian militaries say the drones were most likely Ukrainian. The ship is run by a company that handles transport for Russia's defence ministry and is under US sanctions.
    • On the 22nd there was also an explosion on a Russian ship docked at the naval base in Benghazi, eastern Libya. The eastern-based government said it was probably a drone attack and that ten people, Russians and Libyans, were slightly injured. Neither Russia nor Ukraine has commented.
    Why it matters: the fighting is spreading to seas far from Ukraine.
    Sources: US News (Reuters) / The Star / France 24 / ERR (Estonia) / NBC News / CNN (Libya) / Al Jazeera (Libya) / US News (Libya) / YouTube (TRT World) / YouTube (TRT World, Dymer) / YouTube (TRT World, Benghazi)
  3. 3
    Indonesia: big fire at a sweets factory leaves dozens missingLittle covered in Japan
    • A fire broke out on the 21st at a confectionery factory in Serang, Banten province, on the island of Java. On the 22nd police removed 24 body bags from the burned site. One bag may not hold just one person, so the number of people who died is not yet clear.
    • The factory says that of the 65 workers involved, 24 have gone home and four are being treated. Twenty-nine are missing and the whereabouts of eight more have not been confirmed.
    • The fire was so intense that the victims cannot be identified by sight, so police are collecting DNA from relatives. Results are expected to take two to three weeks. The cause is under investigation, and the search resumes on the morning of the 23rd.
    Why it matters: questions will now be asked about whether the workplace was kept safe.
    Sources: New Straits Times / The Globe and Mail / Euronews / TimesLIVE (Reuters) / YouTube (TRT World)
  4. 4
    France: protests over the cost of living for a sixth weekend; police fire tear gasLittle covered in Japan
    • On the 21st people marched across France against high prices and underfunded public services. It was the sixth weekend of protests in a row. Tens of thousands took part, but fewer than the week before.
    • In Paris, riot police used tear gas after nightfall to clear a square. In Nantes, police used tear gas and rubber bullets after protesters tried to blockade a shopping centre.
    • Al Jazeera reports that it began with students protesting over crumbling school buildings and a shortage of teachers. Unions and workers then joined, and it became a national movement. The government wants to cut $54 billion from next year's budget.
    Why it matters: anger over rising prices is spreading from students to working people.
    Sources: The Local France / AnewZ / Al Jazeera (Nov 5) / CNBC (Nov 8) / YouTube (Al Jazeera English) / YouTube (TRT World)
  5. 5
    South Korea: education minister resigns after confusion over anti-cheating steps for the university entrance exam
    • South Korea's Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin offered to resign on the 22nd, taking responsibility for the confusion over the College Scholastic Ability Test. President Lee Jae Myung accepted.
    • The ministry first focused on smart glasses. But after the top scorer in the tax accountant exam admitted cheating with a hidden camera and AI, it kept adding measures just before the test: stickers to cover clothing buttons, closer checks of glasses and watches, and blocking public Wi-Fi around test centres.
    • The ministry says 98 test takers were caught cheating with smart devices (another report says 111).
    Why it matters: the way exams are run has not caught up with cheating that uses AI and tiny devices.
    Sources: The Korea Times / Korea JoongAng Daily / Xinhua / Khaama Press

Today's Mindfulness Let's Practice Mindfulness

Three minutes with the soles of your feet, for when you feel "there is nothing I can do"

This morning brought news of climate talks that fell apart, of fires, and of villages attacked at night. Reading it, you may have felt drained, thinking "there is nothing I can do." Feeling that way is proof that you are living in the world.
For just three minutes, bring your attention, which has been turned outward, back to the place where you are right now.

  1. Sit on a chair or stand where you are. Breathe out slowly and fully, three times.
  2. Bring your attention to the soles of your feet: the heels, the arches, the base of the toes. Feel each place that touches the floor, one by one.
  3. Look around and silently name three things you can see: "window", "cup", "my hand". Then find two sounds you can hear.
  4. If the thought "there is nothing I can do" comes up, notice "I am having that thought now," and return to the soles of your feet. You do not need to make the thought go away.
  5. Finally, bring to mind just one small thing your own hands can do later today: making a cup of tea, saying "thank you" to someone. Breathe out once, and finish.

Whenever big, faraway things carry your heart off, come back as often as you need to the soles of your feet and to one thing within reach.
