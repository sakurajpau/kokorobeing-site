Japan top 5 little covered by the big networks, but worth knowing

  1. 1
    After the accident off Henoko, about 70% of schools “checked or reviewed” their off-campus activities; the education ministry releases a survey
    In March this year, a boat carrying high school students on a school trip overturned off Henoko in Nago, Okinawa Prefecture, and two people died. On the 24th, the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology released the results of a survey of schools across Japan that followed this accident. About 70% of schools, 23,810 in all, had checked or reviewed the content of school trips and similar activities from the point of view of “whether they are politically neutral.” 72.6% of schools had checked their crisis management manual (their guide for what to do in danger). Some people say this may make it harder to hold activities for learning about peace, but Education Minister Seki rejected that view. Why it is worth knowing: it concerns both keeping children safe and protecting children's chances to learn about society.
    Sources: Okinawa Times / TBS NEWS DIG (Yahoo! News Japan) / Jiji Press (Yahoo! News Japan)
  2. 2
    Japan will pay its money to the International Criminal Court (ICC) early, to prepare for the hardship caused by US sanctions
    On the 24th, Prime Minister Takaichi met Tomoko Akane, president of the International Criminal Court (an international court that tries war crimes and similar cases), and told her that Japan will pay all of its 2027 contribution early, within this year. The US placed sanctions on President Akane in August and later on the court itself. This is making bank transactions difficult, and the court may become unable to pay its staff. Japan is the country that pays the most; in 2026 it paid about 15% of the total, about 4.6 billion yen. President Akane called it “a truly bold decision.” Why it is worth knowing: this is Japan using money to support the world's system of “deciding by law, not by force.”
    Sources: Tokyo Shimbun / Jiji Press (Infoseek) / Newsweek Japan (Reuters) / GMA News (overseas report)

It brings in a system where permission must be obtained in advance to acquire such land. It is expected to become law in the current session of parliament. Food and health from Dr Toshiaki Yoshino's videos

Dr Yoshino: why do people with cancer lose weight even though they are eating?
In a video on November 24, Dr Toshiaki Yoshino says the following. The body normally burns glucose in the “mitochondria” inside cells to make a lot of energy. According to Dr Yoshino, cancer cells cannot use this system well and can make only a little energy from the same amount of glucose. He explains that because of this the body runs short of energy, and “gluconeogenesis,” in which the body breaks down its own muscle and fat to make sugar, goes ahead, so the person loses weight even while eating. This wasted, thin state is called “cachexia.” Dr Yoshino says, “If you get cancer, you must not lose weight,” and he is against people starting to fast or cutting out meat completely on their own judgment. His view is that it is important to learn from ways of eating that have continued since long ago.
These are Dr Yoshino's own views (we have not checked them). Please talk to your own doctor when making decisions about your body, diet or treatment.
Sources: YouTube (吉野敏明チャンネル)

Today's Mindfulness Let's Practice Mindfulness

Three minutes feeling the soles of your feet, after news about having a place to go home to

This morning brought stories of people who returned to their village and found their homes destroyed, and of people whose homes have become like prisons. Reading them, your heart may have felt unsettled or heavy. It is natural to feel that way.
For just three minutes, let's come back to the place where you are right now, starting from the soles of your feet.

  1. Sit on a chair, or stand where you are. Place the soles of both feet on the floor.
  2. Notice the feeling of your soles touching the floor. Your heels, your toes, your arches. Feel the hardness, the warmth or the coolness, just as it is.
  3. Breathe in, and breathe out. With each out-breath, feel the weight of your body being given to the floor. Do this for five breaths.
  4. If the news comes into your mind, notice, "I was thinking just now," and bring your attention back to the soles of your feet. It is fine however many times it comes up.
  5. Finally, look around you. Notice the room you are in now, the floor, the walls and the roof. Take one breath, and finish.

When your mind has gone far away, come back to the soles of your feet. "Here and now" is always under your feet.
